\section{Does the Score Order the Queue?}
\label{sec:ordering}

\subsection{Attainment against a perfect classifier}

Table~\ref{tab:attain} reports mean time-to-first-response for gold-High
tickets. FIFO degrades sharply with load, as a queue with no triage must. The
score tracks the oracle closely at every load, attaining 97.1\% of the achievable
improvement at $\rho = 0.85$ and 99.0\% at $\rho = 1.05$.

\begin{table}[!tb]
\caption{Mean wait for gold-High tickets, minutes, 200 seeds. Attainment is
$(\mathrm{FIFO}-\mathrm{TUS})/(\mathrm{FIFO}-\mathrm{oracle})$.}
\label{tab:attain}
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrl@{}}
\toprule
$\rho$ & FIFO & TUS & Oracle & Attainment \\
\midrule
0.85 & 5.78  & 1.13 & 0.99 & \textbf{0.971} [0.966, 0.977] \\
0.95 & 16.76 & 1.54 & 1.30 & \textbf{0.985} [0.982, 0.987] \\
1.05 & 52.46 & 1.98 & 1.47 & \textbf{0.990} [0.988, 0.992] \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The remaining headroom is about a tenth of a minute. Within this workload,
\textbf{further classifier accuracy cannot buy much}, because the gap between our
noisy labels and perfect ones is nearly closed already by consuming the noisy
labels properly. Section~\ref{sec:accuracy} tests that directly by substituting a
weaker classifier.

\subsection{Rank correlation selects the wrong system}

The obvious way to score an ordering is a rank correlation against the gold
ordering. On our data it is actively misleading (Table~\ref{tab:rank}).

\begin{table}[!tb]
\caption{Ordering quality on test, ordered once. The argmax-tier policy has the
best rank correlation and the worst queue head.}
\label{tab:rank}
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
System & Kendall $\tau_b$ & nDCG@10 & P(High)@10 \\
\midrule
Predicted tier (argmax)  & \textbf{0.822} & 0.351 & 0.50 \\
TUS (LaBSE)              & 0.686 & \textbf{1.000} & \textbf{1.00} \\
Priority posterior only  & 0.690 & \textbf{1.000} & \textbf{1.00} \\
Oracle (gold tier)       & 1.000 & \textbf{1.000} & \textbf{1.00} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The argmax-tier policy has the highest $\tau_b$ of any system and the worst queue
head: half of the first ten tickets an agent opens are not urgent, against a base
rate of 9.5\%. The explanation is structural. Kendall's $\tau$ rewards getting
the global ordering right, and a 3-valued tier score matches a 3-valued gold
label well. But within the top tier every ticket ties, so the order an agent
actually experiences is arbitrary.

A queue is consumed from the top, and $\tau$ averages over the whole list.
Selecting on it would have picked exactly the wrong system. This is the concrete
justification for evaluating triage in queue terms rather than with a ranking
correlation, and it is why we lead with Precision@$k$ over gold-High, which an
operations owner can read without a statistics background.
