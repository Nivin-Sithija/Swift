\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Ticket triage.} Deployed systems predict a per-ticket label and report
classification metrics: escalation risk over 2.5M IBM tickets
\cite{montgomery2018escalation}, developer assignment for bug reports
\cite{mani2019deeptriage}, and an industrial incident deployment
\cite{lee2017industrialtriage}. None asks what the prediction is worth once
tickets compete for a finite number of agents. BANKING77
\cite{casanueva2020banking77} supplies our English track and its human intent
labels.

\textbf{Multilingual encoders.} XLM-R \cite{conneau2020xlmr} and LaBSE
\cite{feng2022labse} transfer across many languages; MuRIL
\cite{khanuja2021muril} and IndicBERT \cite{kakwani2020indicnlpsuite} narrow the
target set to Indian languages, TwHIN-BERT \cite{zhang2023twhinbert} to social
media, and mmBERT \cite{marone2025mmbert} widens it again. Transfer is almost
always measured on native-script data, because that is what pretraining corpora
and benchmarks contain, so the distinction we draw in Section~\ref{sec:script}
is one these benchmarks cannot make. CANINE \cite{clark2022canine} removes the
failure mode architecturally by operating on Unicode code points.

\textbf{Romanized South Asian text.} Chakravarthi et al.\
\cite{chakravarthi2020tamilmix} released human-typed Tanglish, the reference
point that makes our synthetic romanization an optimistic bound. Sinhala
resources are thinner \cite{desilva2019sinhalasurvey}: work covers
back-transliteration \cite{sumanathilaka2026swabhasha}, Sinhala-specific
pretraining \cite{aravinda2025sinllama}, and script effects in Sri Lankan text
\cite{rajapakse2026script}. Parallel banking corpora across these renderings
have not been released.

\textbf{Labels and scheduling.} Two of our label columns are model-generated, so
we report measured ceilings, following the treatment of benchmark label error in
\cite{northcutt2021labelerrors}. For ordering we apply the $c\mu$ rule
\cite{cox1961queues}, its generalisation to convex cost
\cite{vanmieghem1995generalized}, and Argon and Ziya's result that ordering by a
posterior beats ordering by its argmax \cite{argon2009priority}. Because a
ticket carries three labels, we compare binary relevance against classifier
chains \cite{read2009classifierchains}, probabilistic chains
\cite{dembczynski2010pcc}, and stacking \cite{wolpert1992stacked}.
